The first part, called **G1 phase** is marked by synthesis of various [enzymes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzyme "Enzyme") that are required for DNA replication. The second part of the cell cycle is the **S phase**, where [DNA replication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_replication "DNA replication") produces two identical sets of [chromosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromosomes "Chromosomes"). The third part is the **G2 phase** in which a significant [protein synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_synthesis "Protein synthesis") occurs, mainly involving the production of [microtubules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microtubules "Microtubules") that are required during the process of division, called [mitosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitosis "Mitosis"). The PNS consists of the [nerves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve "Nerve") and [ganglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganglia "Ganglia") outside the [brain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain "Brain") and [spinal cord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinal_cord "Spinal cord").[[1]](https://ai.google.com/research/NaturalQuestions/databrowser#cite_note-1) The main function of the PNS is to connect the CNS to the limbs and [organs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_(anatomy) "Organ (anatomy)"), essentially serving as a relay between the brain and spinal cord and the rest of the body.[[2]](https://ai.google.com/research/NaturalQuestions/databrowser#cite_note-2) Unlike the CNS, the PNS is not protected by the [vertebral column](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebral_column "Vertebral column") and [skull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skull "Skull"), or by the [blood–brain barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood%E2%80%93brain_barrier "Blood–brain barrier"), which leaves it exposed to [toxins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toxin "Toxin") and mechanical injuries. The second cranial nerve is not a true peripheral nerve but a tract of the [diencephalon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diencephalon "Diencephalon").[[3]](https://ai.google.com/research/NaturalQuestions/databrowser#cite_note-3)[Cranial nerve ganglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cranial_nerve_ganglia "Cranial nerve ganglia") originated in the CNS. However, the remaining ten cranial nerve [axons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axon "Axon") extend beyond the brain and are therefore considered part of the PNS. [[4]](https://ai.google.com/research/NaturalQuestions/databrowser#cite_note-White2008-4) The autonomic nervous system is an involuntary control of smooth [muscle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscle "Muscle") and [glands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gland "Gland"). The connection between CNS and organs allows the system to be in two different functional states: [sympathetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system") and [parasympathetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasympathetic_nervous_system "Parasympathetic nervous system"). | PNS |
