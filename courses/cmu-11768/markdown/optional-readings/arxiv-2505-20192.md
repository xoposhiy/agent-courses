Abstract

While Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is the pre-
vailing method for equipping Large Language
Models (LLMs) with function calling capabili-
ties, its effectiveness is often compromised by
two critical challenges: 1) Imbalanced Train-
ing Signals, where lengthy Chain-of-Thought
(CoT) reasoning tokens dominate the training
signals over concise function calls in the learn-
ing objective, and 2) Imbalanced Data Hard-
ness, characterized by a scarcity of hard train-
ing examples. To overcome these limitations,
we propose Balanced Supervised Fine-tuning
(BalanceSFT), a novel framework incorporates
two key components: a Self-adjusted Signal
Balancing (SSB) loss that employs a learnable
hyperparameter to dynamically adjust the token
contributions of CoT reasoning and function
calls, together with a Hard Data Re-sampling
(HDR) strategy that establishes a feedback loop
to selectively generate new, high-quality com-
plex data guided by model errors. Extensive ex-
periments demonstrate the effectiveness of our
proposed BalanceSFT framework. With Bal-
anceSFT, a 7B model achieves function calling
performance on par with state-of-the-art giants
like GPT-4o. Our code, models, and dataset are
open-sourced.

Related Work

Function Calling for LLMs. The ability of LLMs
to interact with external tools and APIs through
function calling has emerged as a crucial aspect of
their practical utility (Wang et al., 2024b; Singh
et al., 2024; Patil et al., 2024). This capability al-
lows LLMs to transcend the limitations of solely
processing and generating text, enabling them to
ground their responses in real-world data and au-
tomate complex tasks (Wang et al., 2024a; Ka-
vathekar et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025a). Early
explorations in this area focused on enabling LLMs
to understand descriptions of functions and gener-
ate the necessary calls with appropriate parame-
ters (Wang et al., 2023; Zheng et al., 2023). By
training LLMs on datasets specifically curated for
function calling scenarios, these methods aim to
instill a deeper understanding of function seman-
tics and parameter requirements (Prabhakar et al.,
2025; Zeng et al., 2025b). The quality and diversity
of these fine-tuning datasets play a critical role in
the resulting performance of the LLMs in function
calling tasks. RL has also been explored as a means
to optimize LLMs for function calling (Zhang et al.,
2025b; Qian et al., 2025). RL-based approaches
often involve defining reward functions that incen-
tivize the generation of correct and executable func-
tion calls. While RL offers the potential to directly
optimize for task success, it still severe sparse re-
ward and the lack of effective data (Qu et al., 2025;
Zeng et al., 2025a).
Improving Reasoning Capability via SFT. The
integration of CoT with function calling mech-
anisms holds substantial promise for enhancing
the accuracy and reliability of tool-use (Lu et al.,
2024; Kokane et al.; Chen et al., 2024). By reason-
ing through the user’s request and identifying the
most appropriate course of action, including the
selection of relevant tools and their parameters, the
model can make more informed decisions about
function invocation (Li, 2025; Shen, 2024). The
explicit reasoning process provided by CoT can
also improve the transparency and interpretability
of the model’s actions (Wu et al., 2025; Dong et al.,
2025). However, the integration of CoT into the
training process for function calling introduces a
unique challenge related to the design of the loss
function (Yang et al., 2025b). Traditional training
methodologies, primarily SFT, typically treat the
entire generated sequence (including the reasoning
process and the final function call) uniformly when

lead to an imbalance, where the potentially lengthy

chains (Agarwal et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025b),

for a succinct and precise function call necessitates
a more nuanced approach to loss function design
that can effectively balance these two critical as-
pects of the task. Our work addresses this challenge
by introducing a Self-adjusted Signal Balancing ap-
proach that explicitly considers the different roles
and importance of the reasoning process and the
final function execution during training.

3
Method

In this section, we elaborate on our proposed
Balanced Supervised Fine-Tuning (BalanceSFT)
framework, which is designed to address the dual
challenges of Imbalanced Training Signals and Im-
balanced Data Hardness.

3.1
Overview

The overall architecture of our BalanceSFT frame-
work is illustrated in Figure 2. It consists of two
core components that work in synergy: 1) A Self-
adjusted Signal Balancing (SSB) loss that dynami-
cally balances the learning signals between lengthy
Chain-of-Thought reasoning tokens and concise
function call tokens during training, and a Hard
Data Re-sampling (HDR) strategy that establishes
a feedback loop to selectively generate and utilize
high-quality complex data based on model errors.
These two components address the signal imbal-
ance and data hardness imbalance, respectively,
forming a comprehensive solution for robust func-
tion calling capability.

4.2
Data Preparation

We construct two CoT datasets: (1) Strategy CoT,
generated by GPT-4o following prescribed reason-
ing strategies, and (2) Natural CoT, generated by
QwQ-32B (Team, 2024) based on its natural rea-
soning process (implemented with vLLM (Kwon
et al., 2023), temperature = 0.1). We conduct stan-
dard SFT on Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Inst using each
dataset. As shown in Table 3, we observe that Nat-
ural CoT consistently outperforms Strategy CoT.
Therefore, we adopt Natural CoT for cold-start
data generation and employ self-inference in the
Hard Data Re-sampling (HDR) stage to maintain
model-consistent reasoning patterns.

5
Conclusion

In this work, we identify two critical yet overlooked
challenges in standard SFT for function calling: the
imbalance in training signals between lengthy rea-
soning and concise function calls, and the scarcity
of hard training data. To address these issues, we
propose BalanceSFT, a novel framework that in-
tegrates a Self-adjusted Signal Balancing (SSB)
loss and a Hard Data Re-sampling (HDR) strategy.
The SSB loss dynamically recalibrates the learn-
ing focus between reasoning and execution, while
HDR iteratively enriches the training set with high-
quality complex examples. Extensive experiments
demonstrate that BalanceSFT enables a 7B model
to achieve function calling performance compet-
itive with state-of-the-art giants like GPT-4o on
BFCL and other benchmarks. Our work provides
a simple yet effective pathway toward more robust
and generalizable LLM-based tool use.

Limitations

Despite its strong performance, BalanceSFT has
two major limitations. First, the effectiveness of
the SSB loss depends on the initial quality and
structure of the CoT data, which may limit its ap-
plicability in domains where high-quality reason-
ing annotations are scarce. Second, while HDR
improves data hardness, it relies on an ensemble
of LLM judges for quality control, which intro-
duces computational cost and potential bias from
the judge models.
