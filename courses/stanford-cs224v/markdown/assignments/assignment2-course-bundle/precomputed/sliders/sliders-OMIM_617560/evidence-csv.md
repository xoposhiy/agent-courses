## 5. Phenotypic Stratification by Age, Sex, Variant Type, and Position

### Age of Onset Stratification
SPAX8 presents across two major clinical trajectories that reflect developmental timing rather than distinct genetic etiologies (Chelban et al., 2017, DOI: 10.1016/j.ajhg.2017.03.011; Chelban et al., 2020, DOI: 10.1111/ene.14082) [F3]:
- **Early Infantile / Neonatal Presentation:** Characterized by early-onset pendular or horizontal nystagmus (HP:0000639) evident within the first 6 months of life, accompanied by marked axial hypotonia (HP:0001252), failure to achieve head control (4/5 individuals in Dorboz et al., 2017), severe global psychomotor delay (HP:0001263), and rapid transition to spastic quadriparesis (HP:0001257) with pseudobulbar dysarthria and dysphagia [F3, F9]. Developmental motor regression (HP:0002376) is frequently observed in this subgroup (Baldi et al., 2018) [F9, F10].
- **Late Childhood / Juvenile Presentation:** Characterized by relatively preserved early motor milestones (e.g., achieving independent walking), followed by insidious-onset progressive cerebellar ataxia (HP:0001251), spastic paraparesis, slurred speech, and loss of ambulation during late childhood or adolescence (Chelban et al., 2017) [F2, F3]. Cognition may remain relatively spared [F8, F10].

### Sex Distribution
SPAX8 is inherited in an autosomal recessive manner. Across all reported cohorts (Chelban et al., 2017; Baldi et al., 2018; Chelban et al., 2020; Bereshneh et al., 2020), disease penetrance and clinical severity show no statistically significant sex bias, affecting male and female siblings with equal frequency and progression rates [F1, F2, F10].

### Variant Type and Position: Refutation of Binary Genotype-Phenotype Models
Early hypotheses proposed that biallelic protein-truncating variants (nonsense, frameshift, splice-site) caused severe infantile hypomyelinating leukodystrophy, whereas missense substitutions in the conserved homeobox domain resulted in milder childhood-onset spastic ataxia with preserved ambulation. 

**This genotype-phenotype correlation has been definitively refuted by cohort analyses** (Baldi et al., 2018, DOI: 10.1111/cge.13221; Bereshneh et al., 2020, PMID: 32004679; Chelban et al., 2018, GeneReviews NBK531509) [F16]. Severe infantile presentations with profound developmental arrest, early motor regression, and permanent lack of ambulation have been documented in individuals harboring homozygous missense mutations within the homeodomain (e.g., p.Phe167Leu) (Baldi et al., 2018; Bereshneh et al., 2020) [F16]. Consequently, clinicians cannot predict mild versus severe disease course based solely on whether a variant is missense or truncating [F16].

## 6. Literature Divergences, Contested Hypotheses, and Resolving Data

Several discrepancies exist in published literature regarding cohort counts, neuroimaging obligacy, cognitive penetrance, and clinical frequencies. These issues are detailed below alongside their resolving evidence:

### 1. Foundational Discovery Cohort Size Discrepancy
- *Literature Error / Contested Claim:* Multiple secondary literature sources and review articles erroneously cite the original discovery publication by Chelban et al. (2017, PMID: 28430856 / PMID: 28575651) as describing 14 affected individuals from 8 families [F13].
- *Resolving Evidence:* The foundational discovery cohort published by Chelban et al. (2017, *Am J Hum Genet*, PMID: 28575651) comprised **7 affected individuals from 3 unrelated families** [F1]. The expanded count arose from later multi-center aggregations, including Chelban et al. (2020, DOI: 10.1111/ene.14082), which identified 11 new cases from 8 new families, and the GeneReviews synthesis (Chelban et al., 2018) reviewing 25 individuals across 13 families [F1, F2, F8].

### 2. Obligate Central Hypomyelination versus Non-Leukodystrophic Presentation
- *Contested Claim:* Diffuse cerebral hypomyelination (HP:0003429) is a universal, obligate neuroimaging hallmark present in 100% of biallelic *NKX6-2* patients [F11, F13].
- *Resolving Evidence:* Diffuse hypomyelination is **not** obligate. In the discovery cohort (Chelban et al., 2017, PMID: 28575651), only Family 3 presented with diffuse brain hypomyelination; affected individuals in Families 1 and 2 (childhood-onset spastic ataxia) exhibited **isolated cerebellar atrophy (HP:0001272) without overt hypomyelination** (Chelban et al., 2017, 2020) [F2, F11, F13]. Approximately 20% to 30% of reported individuals display predominant cerebellar vermian atrophy without classic leukodystrophy [F2, F11]. Treating hypomyelination as a mandatory inclusion criterion leads to underdiagnosis of childhood-onset SPAX8.

### 3. Cognitive Impairment: Severe Intellectual Disability versus Cognitive Preservation
- *Contested Claim:* Intellectual disability is universally moderate-to-severe across all SPAX8 patients [F8, F10].
- *Resolving Evidence:* This divergence stems directly from **ascertainment bias**. Kindreds identified through intellectual disability and congenital encephalopathy sequencing pipelines (e.g., Anazi et al., 2017, DOI: 10.1007/s00439-017-1843-2; Lan et al., 2025) report profound cognitive deficiency and developmental arrest [F8]. Conversely, cohorts ascertained through adult or pediatric movement disorder and spastic ataxia clinics (Chelban et al., 2017, 2018) describe patients with preserved intellectual faculties and normal speech comprehension despite severe motor disability [F8, F10]. Cognitive impairment is therefore variable and correlates with early-onset developmental disruption rather than representing an obligate primary feature across all ages.

### 4. Epileptic Seizures and Leukodystrophic Dissociation
- *Contested Claim:* Epilepsy (HP:0001250) selectively tracks with severe diffuse cerebral hypomyelination [F15].
- *Resolving Evidence:* Chelban et al. (2020, DOI: 10.1111/ene.14082) demonstrated that epilepsy dissociates from neuroimaging severity: seizures can occur in individuals lacking overt hypomyelination, while patients with extensive diffuse hypomyelination may remain completely seizure-free [F5, F15]. Seizures occurred in 2 of 6 families evaluated by Baldi et al. (2018), confirming that epilepsy is an unpredictable secondary feature (33%) rather than an obligate manifestation of white matter pathology [F9].

### 5. Developmental Regression Prevalence
- *Contested Claim:* Developmental regression (HP:0002376) is rare, occurring in fewer than 15–20% of patients [F9].
- *Resolving Evidence:* Baldi et al. (2018, DOI: 10.1111/cge.13221) documented developmental and motor regression in **3 of 6 families (50%)**, and Bereshneh et al. (2020) observed motor regression in 2 of 2 affected siblings, demonstrating that regression is an under-recognized hallmark in early-onset SPAX8 rather than an exceptional outlier [F9].

### 6. Occurrence of Peripheral Axonal Neuropathy
- *Contested Claim:* Peripheral nervous system involvement is entirely absent in SPAX8 [F14].
- *Resolving Evidence:* Bereshneh et al. (2020, PMID: 32004679) and Almatrafi et al. (2020, DOI: 10.1002/jgm.3196) reported affected siblings with homozygous *NKX6-2* mutations who developed motor-predominant **peripheral axonal neuropathy (HP:0003477) with wrist drop and foot drop** (2 of 4 individuals assessed) [F4, F7, F14]. While electrophysiology confirms preserved peripheral myelin sheaths (excluding primary demyelinating polyneuropathy), secondary distal axonal degeneration represents a genuine, low-frequency (<10%) manifestation of SPAX8 [F4, F7, F12].

## 7. Cross-Cutting Patterns and Clinical Synthesis

Integrating the clinical, radiological, and genetic data reveals several cross-cutting pathophysiological principles that inform diagnostic practice:

1. **Dissociation Between Cardinal Syndromic Hallmarks and Neuroimaging Penetrance:** Diffuse hypomyelination, while eponymous, is non-obligate. The homeobox factor NKX6-2 plays distinct temporal roles in forebrain oligodendroglial myelination versus cerebellar circuit maintenance. In older-onset patients, cerebellar degeneration may proceed in the absence of overt forebrain dysmyelination (Chelban et al., 2017, 2020) [F2, F11]. Clinicians evaluating spastic ataxia panels lacking leukodystrophy must keep *NKX6-2* on their differential for isolated cerebellar atrophy [F2].
2. **Decoupling of Oculomotor and Paroxysmal Manifestations from Structural White Matter:** Nystagmus is present in virtually all SPAX8 patients regardless of whether white matter is intact or severely hypomyelinated (Chelban et al., 2018) [F6]. Similarly, seizure susceptibility occurs independently of leukodystrophy severity (Chelban et al., 2020) [F5]. This suggests *NKX6-2* functions in inhibitory interneuron circuit development and cerebellar-vestibular integration independently of its structural role in myelin maintenance.
3. **Failure of Binary Truncating/Missense Predictions in Homeodomain Proteins:** Classic genotype-phenotype paradigms (truncating = null/severe; missense = hypomorphic/mild) fail in SPAX8 (Baldi et al., 2018; Bereshneh et al., 2020) [F16]. Specific homeodomain missense substitutions (such as p.Phe167Leu) likely exert dominant-negative, chromatin-trapping, or structural destabilizing effects that equal or exceed the pathogenic impact of complete genetic nulls [F16].
4. **Secondary Axonopathy Mimicry:** The appearance of wrist drop and foot drop in rare kindreds reflects length-dependent axonal degeneration secondary to axo-glial signaling disruption rather than a primary peripheral demyelinating polyneuropathy (Bereshneh et al., 2020; Buttermore et al., 2013) [F4, F7, F12].
