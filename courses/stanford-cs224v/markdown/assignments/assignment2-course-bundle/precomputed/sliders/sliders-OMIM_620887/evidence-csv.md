### Diagnostic Discriminators: Differentiating MMDS9B from Overlapping Syndromes

When evaluating a patient with complex neurosensory or neuromuscular decline, several primary differential diagnostic categories emerge (F3; F5). The following phenotypic features allow clinical discrimination:

#### 1. Differentiation from Primary Mitochondrial Optic Neuropathies (LHON and OPA1-DOA)
* **Overlapping Presentation:** Bilateral central visual loss, optic disc pallor, and visual field defects (F2; F5).
* **Discriminating Features:** 
  * *Retinal Dystrophy and Photoreceptor Dysfunction:* Leber hereditary optic neuropathy (LHON) and *OPA1*-related dominant optic atrophy (DOA) predominantly spare photoreceptors and retinal vasculature (F5). By contrast, FDXR deficiency routinely causes rod-cone retinal dystrophy, early-onset severe retinal dystrophy, or congenital amaurosis (F2; F5).
  * *Retinal Vascular Attenuation / Silver Wiring:* Prominent vessel attenuation appearing as thin white threads or ghost vessels ('silver wiring'), confirmed by vessel occlusion on fluorescein angiography, is present in 60–100% of ophthalmologically examined FDXR patients (3/5 in Wei et al., 2023; 11/11 in Yi et al., 2023) (F5). In a cohort of 826 families with hereditary optic neuropathy (nHON), concurrent retinal degeneration was seen in only 17.57%, whereas 78.79% had isolated optic atrophy (Zheng et al., 2025, [10.1093/brain/awae316](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awae316)) (F5).
* **Diagnostic Confidence:** **High**. The coexistence of profound retinal vessel attenuation and rod-cone retinal dystrophy alongside optic atrophy strongly argues against isolated LHON or *OPA1*-DOA (F2; F5).

### Diagnostic Discriminators: Differentiating MMDS9B from Overlapping Syndromes

When evaluating a patient with complex neurosensory or neuromuscular decline, several primary differential diagnostic categories emerge (F3; F5). The following phenotypic features allow clinical discrimination:

#### 1. Differentiation from Primary Mitochondrial Optic Neuropathies (LHON and OPA1-DOA)
* **Overlapping Presentation:** Bilateral central visual loss, optic disc pallor, and visual field defects (F2; F5).
* **Discriminating Features:** 
  * *Retinal Dystrophy and Photoreceptor Dysfunction:* Leber hereditary optic neuropathy (LHON) and *OPA1*-related dominant optic atrophy (DOA) predominantly spare photoreceptors and retinal vasculature (F5). By contrast, FDXR deficiency routinely causes rod-cone retinal dystrophy, early-onset severe retinal dystrophy, or congenital amaurosis (F2; F5).
  * *Retinal Vascular Attenuation / Silver Wiring:* Prominent vessel attenuation appearing as thin white threads or ghost vessels ('silver wiring'), confirmed by vessel occlusion on fluorescein angiography, is present in 60–100% of ophthalmologically examined FDXR patients (3/5 in Wei et al., 2023; 11/11 in Yi et al., 2023) (F5). In a cohort of 826 families with hereditary optic neuropathy (nHON), concurrent retinal degeneration was seen in only 17.57%, whereas 78.79% had isolated optic atrophy (Zheng et al., 2025, [10.1093/brain/awae316](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awae316)) (F5).
* **Diagnostic Confidence:** **High**. The coexistence of profound retinal vessel attenuation and rod-cone retinal dystrophy alongside optic atrophy strongly argues against isolated LHON or *OPA1*-DOA (F2; F5).

#### 2. Differentiation from Upstream Multiple Mitochondrial Dysfunctions Syndromes (MMDS1–3)
* **Overlapping Presentation:** Autosomal recessive mitochondrial Fe-S cluster assembly defects affecting lipoate- and Fe-S-dependent enzymes (F3; F4).
* **Discriminating Features:**
  * Upstream syndromes—MMDS1 (*NFU1*), MMDS2 (*BOLA3*), and MMDS3 (*IBA57*)—classically manifest in neonates or young infants with fulminant lactic acidosis, early pulmonary hypertension, cystic cavitating leukodystrophy, and early death (F3; F4). 
  * In contrast, MMDS9B (*FDXR*) presents primarily as a progressive sensorial neuropathy (auditory neuropathy spectrum disorder [ANSD] and optic atrophy) during childhood, adolescence, or adulthood, without catastrophic infantile systemic collapse (F3; F4).
* **Diagnostic Confidence:** **High**. Lack of infantile pulmonary hypertension and absence of neonatal cystic leukodystrophy clearly separates classical MMDS9B from MMDS1–3 (F3; F4).
