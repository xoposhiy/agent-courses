## Phenotypic Discrimination and Differential Diagnosis
To isolate an individual with *SNUPN* deficiency out of a cohort of patients presenting with proximal or proximo-distal weakness, clinicians must rely on specific discriminatory imaging and physical examination signatures (F6, F7).

### Discrimination from Sporadic Inclusion Body Myositis (sIBM)
* **Confused disorder:** Sporadic inclusion body myositis (sIBM).
* **Overlapping features:** Adult onset, proximo-distal weakness pattern, axial muscle involvement, myopathic features with rimmed vacuoles and protein aggregations on muscle biopsy (F2, F7, F8).
* **Discriminating clinical signs:** In sIBM, deep finger flexor weakness (flexor digitorum profundus) is a hallmark early sign present in the vast majority of patients (F7). In adult-onset *SNUPN*-related myopathy, deep finger flexor power is completely preserved (0/2 affected siblings showed deep finger flexor weakness) (N Muelas et al., 2026, 10.1002/acn3.70211) [F7].
* **Discriminating MRI signs:** Muscle MRI in sIBM characteristically spares the sartorius and gracilis muscles while predominantly targeting the quadriceps (rectus femoris, vasti) (FM Cox et al., 2011, 10.1093/rheumatology/ker001) [F7]. Conversely, in *SNUPN* deficiency, the sartorius and gracilis exhibit consistent, early, and selective fatty infiltration along with the vasti and paravertebral muscles across both childhood and adult cases (5/5 assessed patients) (P Iruzubieta et al., 2024; N Muelas et al., 2026) [F6, F7, F10].
* **Confidence of distinction:** **High** (supported by clinical testing and skeletal muscle MRI) [F7].

### Discrimination from Other Recessive Limb-Girdle Muscular Dystrophies
* **Confused disorders:** Calpainopathy (LGMDR1/LGMD2A), dysferlinopathy (LGMDR2/LGMD2B), sarcoglycanopathies (LGMDR3-R6), and collagen VI-related dystrophies (F8, F13).
* **Discriminating signs:** 
  1. *Axial muscle predilection:* Severe, early, and selective paravertebral muscle atrophy and fatty infiltration are present in LGMDR29 across both childhood-onset (5/5 patients) and adult-onset (2/2 patients) cases (F6, F10).
  2. *Subclinical cerebellar atrophy:* Demonstrating cerebellar atrophy on cranial MRI in a patient with muscular dystrophy strongly discriminates LGMDR29 from classical sarcoglycanopathies, dysferlinopathies, and calpainopathies (F1, F3, F4).
  3. *Histopathological myofibrillar alterations:* Presence of myofibrillar-like changes, Z-disc disorganization, and ectopic protein aggregates (e.g., myotilin) alongside standard dystrophic changes distinguishes LGMDR29 from pure sarcolemmal dystrophies (P Iruzubieta et al., 2024, PMID:38366623) [F2].
* **Confidence of distinction:** **Moderate to High** [F1, F6, F7].

### Discrimination from Autosomal Recessive Cerebellar Ataxias
* **Confused disorders:** Recessive spinocerebellar ataxias, Friedreich ataxia, and ataxia with oculomotor apraxia (F1, F4).
* **Discriminating signs:** Presence of elevated serum CK, subclinical dystrophic/myopathic abnormalities on electromyography or muscle biopsy, and selective fatty degeneration of lower limb muscles on skeletal MRI distinguish *SNUPN*-related ataxia from pure primary ataxias (M Okubo et al., 2026, 10.1093/brain/awaf348; N Muelas et al., 2026, 10.1002/acn3.70211) [F2, F4, F5].
* **Confidence of distinction:** **Moderate** [F4, F5].
