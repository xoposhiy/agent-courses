CS329Z Paper Video: Presenting an Agent Paper

CS329Z Staff

Fall 2026

1
Overview

In this assignment, you will record a 10–15 minute video presenting a recent AI agent paper of your
choice. You’ll explain the problem the paper tackles, how the system works, and what evidence
supports it, then give your own critical take. You’ll also go beyond the paper: run the code, walk
through an example, or try a small experiment, and share an insight you couldn’t get from reading
the paper alone.

1.1
At a Glance

• Due: Friday, November 13, 11:59 p.m. PT (Week 8).

• Weight: 10% of your course grade for the video, plus 5% for peer reviews.

• Peer review: you will review two classmates’ videos, and two classmates will review yours.
Details will be released separately.

1.2
Grading

The video is graded out of 70 points across four parts:

• Paper selection & explanation (20 pts): an eligible, well-chosen paper, explained clearly
and accurately (Sections 2 and 3.1).

• Critique & insight (20 pts): your own take: what you agree or disagree with, limitations,
or an interesting question, and how the paper connects to CS329Z (Section 3.2).

• Added value (20 pts): something that goes beyond reading the paper (Section 3.3).

• Clear & engaging presentation (10 pts): organization, pacing, audio, and meeting the
format requirements (Section 3.4).

2
Choosing a Paper

Pick a paper that meets all of the following:

• About AI agents: the paper should be centered on agentic systems: agent architectures or
harnesses, tool use, retrieval, memory, multi-agent systems, prompt/program optimization,
agent evaluation and benchmarks, safety and privacy, coding agents, computer-use or web
agents, proactive agents, and similar.

• Recent: there’s no strict cutoff date, but the paper should be recent. If you’re not sure
whether yours counts, check with a TA.

• A reputable research paper: it should have a method and an evaluation. Peer-reviewed
conference and journal papers are both fine. Blog posts, product announcements, and survey
papers don’t count unless staff approve them.

• Not already covered in class: papers listed as readings (required or additional) for lectures
through Monday, October 19, or discussed by name in those lectures, are not eligible.
Readings for later lectures are fine.

Choosing an ineligible paper without staff approval earns 0 points for paper selection & explanation.

! On arXiv Papers

arXiv preprints are also reasonable, but use discretion since anyone can post to arXiv. If
you choose an arXiv paper, pick one that people actually use and build on. GitHub stars
or citations are good indicators of this.

You don’t need to cover every component or result in the paper, only what your audience needs

3.1
Paper Selection & Explanation (20 pts)

Choose an eligible paper (Section 2) and explain it clearly and accurately.

Requirement (explanation): Explain the paper

1. Citation: At the start of the video, show the paper’s full title, authors, venue/year, and
a link.

2. Problem & motivation: What problem does the paper tackle, and why does it matter?
What did people do before this paper, and where did that fall short?

3. How the system works: Explain the method clearly enough that a classmate could
sketch the agent: the model(s), tools, memory/context, environment, and control loop.

4. Evidence: How do the authors evaluate it? Cover the benchmarks/tasks, metrics, base-
lines, and the key results, and say what those results actually show.

3.2
Critique & Insight (20 pts)

Give your own take on the paper, not just a summary of it.

Requirement (critique): Share your perspective

1. Critical analysis: What do you agree or disagree with? You might discuss limitations,
threats to evaluation validity, likely failure modes, cost/latency, safety or privacy concerns,
or claims the evidence doesn’t fully support. Go beyond the limitations the authors already
list.

2. Connection to CS329Z: Relate the paper to at least one course topic (e.g., context
engineering, RAG, tool use/MCP, memory, multi-agent systems, optimization, evaluation,
safety). How does it support, extend, or challenge what we covered?

3. Takeaways: One or two lessons for someone building agents, plus an interesting open
question or follow-up experiment.

3.3
Added Value (20 pts)

Anyone can summarize a paper, and so can a coding agent. We want you to engage with the work
directly and share something you could not learn just by reading it. Spend a meaningful part of

your video on at least one extension, for example:

• Run the project code: get the released code running and talk about what worked, what
didn’t, and what surprised you.

• Walk through one eval example: step through a single task or trajectory end to end,
showing what the agent actually did.

• Reproduce a result: re-run one number or figure from the paper (even at a smaller scale)

• Run a small experiment: change a prompt, model, tool, or setting and report what
happens.

• Compare with another method: try a baseline or alternative approach on the same
example.

• Demo an implementation: build a minimal version of the key idea, or connect it to a real
agent.

Requirement (extension): Go beyond the paper

1. Do the extension: Show what you did in the video.

2. Explain what you found: What happened, and what does it tell you about the paper?
Does it confirm, complicate, or contradict its claims?

3. Include your work: Add any code or outputs you used to your Gradescope materials.

Small and honest is fine: a failed reproduction with a clear explanation of why is a good
extension.

! No Code Released?

If your paper has no public code, pick an extension that doesn’t need it, such as a minimal
implementation, a hand-traced example, or a comparison against a similar open-source
agent. Check with a TA if you’re unsure.

3.4
Clear & Engaging Presentation (10 pts)

Requirement (presentation): Present it well

1. Length: 10–15 minutes. Staff may stop watching after 15 minutes.

2. Organization & pacing: A logical flow, with time spent in proportion to importance.
Don’t rush your critique or extension, and explain jargon as you go.

3. Audio & visuals: Clear, audible audio (do a short test recording first), and on-screen

How to Submit

Submit through the Paper Video assignment on Gradescope. You can resubmit as often as you like

1. Your video link (unlisted YouTube): Upload the video to YouTube and set visibility

2. The materials you used to create the video: e.g., slides (PDF preferred), plus any
script, notes, code, or demo files you used.

Deliverable: Open your link in a private/incognito window while logged out and confirm it
plays, then submit the link and your materials on Gradescope. Don’t delete or re-upload the
video after the deadline.

! Not Comfortable Posting to YouTube?

If you’re uncomfortable posting to YouTube, even as unlisted, contact the teaching staff
before the deadline and we’ll make accommodations.
