# CSCI 7000-09 Fall 2026: Syllabus


### TEXTBOOK

Selected research papers will be available online through ACM or IEEE Digital Library, or through ArXiV.

### PREREQUISITES

Students should have basic knowledge of Computer Science undergrad classes, including software engineering, programming languages, systems, ML/AI as demonstrated by undergrad courses at CU. This is  **NOT**  an introductory grad course to Software Engineering, for those students we recommend other grad courses such as Foundations of Software Engineering (CSCI 5828). This course assumes you are a competent software engineer already -- we are looking into how you can augment your current skills to include GenAI supported software development.
If you are not sure whether you can attend this course, please consult the instructor (email: danny.dig@colorado.edu)

- Participate in class discussion and activities.

- Complete a term project

### Appropriate use of GenAI

Because this course focuses on building AI agents, thoughtful use of Generative AI is generally encouraged; however, the [Paper Critique assignment](page-papercritiques.md) is specifically designed to develop your own reading, critical-thinking, and writing skills. You must read the entire paper yourself and independently answer the questions in the critique template. AI may be used only as a learning and thinking partner to help fill gaps in your understanding—for example, to explain unfamiliar concepts, provide background on a research area, or help you reason through a difficult topic. AI may not summarize the paper for you, answer the critique questions, generate an outline or draft, or rewrite, edit, polish, or correct any part of your submitted critique. The guiding principle is simple: use AI to increase your understanding, not to replace your thinking. The submitted Paper Critique must be fully authored and polished by you.

### NO LAPTOP POLICY -- for when class is held in person on Campus

Several studies and anecdotal experience shows that laptops are a distraction in the classroom - they disrupt learning, lower the performance of
students who use them, and even distract the other students. Here is an article that discusses some of the issues:
[article1](../optional-readings/ref-ba3f8201fe75.md),
[article2](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360131512002254?np=y).
Students may not use laptops,
cell phones, or other electronic devices in this classroom. If you expect this will pose an undue hardship for your learning, please speak with the professor.
