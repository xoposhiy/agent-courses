# CSCI 7000-09 Fall 2026: Paper Critiques


### SUMMARY

We will have two papers discussed per meeting. Each student need to read
all assigned papers and write reports.
However, if you are presenting in a given
meeting, you need not write a report for any of the other papers.

Students should critique the papers to be discussed in class. Do not directly
copy the abstract, introduction, conclusion or any other part of the paper - you
must add value and insight beyond what is already in the paper.
Unless specified otherwise, all paper critiques are due by 12pm the day of the respective class
and should address the following questions (inspired from this excellent resource on how to [Read an Engineering Research paper](../readings/ref-6dfbf2b33b8a.md) by William Griswold):

#### 1. What I liked

What is your analysis of the identified problem, idea and evaluation? Is this a good
idea? What are the most interesting points made? What are the most controversial ideas
or points made? For work that has practical implications, you also want to ask: Is this
really going to work, who would want it, what it will take to give it to them, and when
might it become a reality?

#### 2. What I disliked

What flaws do you perceive in the work?

#### 3. Future directions for this research

Not only what future directions do the authors identify, but what ideas did you come up with
while reading the paper? Sometimes these may be identified as shortcomings or other critiques
in the current work. Examples include, but are not limited to: Can the same problem have a different
solution? Can the same solution be applied to a different problem? Would a bigger evaluation be appropriate?

#### 4. Open questions

What questions are you left with? What questions would you like to raise in an open discussion of the work?
What do you find confusing or difficult to understand? By taking the time to list several, you will be forced
to think more deeply about the work.

#### 5. Most important for class discussion

What are you looking forward to get out of a class discussion based on this paper? What are the most important points you would like to discuss with colleagues and the professor in class?

#### 6. Summary

What is your take-away message from this paper? Sum up the main implication of the paper from your perspective.
This is useful for very quick review and refreshing your memory. It also forces you to try to identify the essence
of the work.

### OPTION TWO: Tool Experience Report

Some students gain more insights using experiential learning: read a paper, download the tool, and use it for your own usecase. Then write a Tool Experience Report -- instead of the Paper Critique format outlined above. After using the tool, write a 1-page, concise reflection on using the tool introduced by the research paper, following this format:

#### 1. Your Experience Using the Tool

What specific task(s) did you perform with the tool?
How easy or difficult was it to install, set up, and use?
Did the tool behave as described in the paper? Mention any differences or surprises you found.

#### 2. Strengths

What are the strongest aspects of this tool (e.g., accuracy, usability, speed, integration, insights)?
What features or design choices did you find especially effective or well thought out?

#### 3. Weaknesses

What limitations or challenges did you encounter (e.g., poor documentation, installation issues, narrow scope, inaccurate results)?
Were there any tasks or contexts where the tool performed poorly or failed to meet expectations?

#### 4. Potential for Practical Impact

How could this tool influence software engineering practice if adopted widely?
What types of developers, teams, or companies would most benefit from it?
What barriers to adoption (e.g., trust, policy, scalability) might stand in the way?

#### 5. Reflection and Recommendations

What did you learn from using the tool that you wouldn’t have learned from reading the paper alone?
What one improvement would you recommend to the tool’s creators?

### Submission Procedure

Submit your paper critique by 12pm on the day of the class on Canvas. By sending your questions earlier than the class time, the presenter will have time to find some other relevant materials that are addressing your questions. Thus, the presenter will be able to better serve you.

### Paper critique policies

- Students are welcome to discuss the papers even before writing the report, but reports should be written individually. CSCI 7000-009 uses the standard ethics code for CS courses.
- Reports are due by 12pm MST on the day of the class (submitting earlier is OK and encouraged)
- You can't have any paper critique sent late; if you need an extension, ask in advance, and you may be assigned a make-up paper.

### AI Use Policy for Paper Critiques

#### AI as a Learning Partner, Not an Author

This course is about building AI agents, so the thoughtful use of Generative AI is not only permitted but encouraged. Learning to work effectively with AI is an important course objective. However, some assignments are designed to develop skills that only improve through independent reading, critical thinking, and writing. The **Paper Critique** assignment is one of those assignments.

#### Allowed Uses of AI

You may use AI **before or during your reading** to deepen your understanding of the paper, provided that AI is helping **you learn**, not doing the assignment for you. Appropriate uses include:

- Asking AI to explain unfamiliar concepts, terminology, or methods mentioned in the paper.
- Requesting background information about a research area to fill gaps in your understanding.
- Using AI as a thinking partner by asking questions that help you reason through complex ideas or compare alternative interpretations.
- Exploring examples, analogies, or visual explanations that improve your understanding of the paper.

These uses are encouraged because they help you learn more effectively and make you a stronger researcher.

#### Prohibited Uses of AI

The **Paper Critique must be entirely your own intellectual work.** AI may **not** be used to create, edit, or improve any part of the submitted critique. This includes, but is not limited to:

- Summarizing the paper for you.
- Answering any of the questions in the critique template.
- Generating outlines, bullet points, or draft text for the critique.
- Rewriting, paraphrasing, or polishing your writing.
- Correcting grammar, style, or wording.
- Editing your critique in any way before submission.

In other words, **no part of the submitted Paper Critique may be authored or refined by AI.**

#### Guiding Principle

**Use AI to increase your understanding, not to replace your thinking.**

The purpose of the Paper Critique is to demonstrate that **you** have carefully read the paper, identified its key ideas, evaluated its strengths and weaknesses, and articulated your own insights. These are essential skills for researchers and engineers, and they can only be developed through deliberate practice.

When deciding whether a particular use of AI is appropriate, ask yourself:

> **Is AI helping me learn more deeply, or is it doing the intellectual work that I am expected to do myself?**

If AI is helping you understand the paper better, it is likely appropriate. If AI is performing the reading, analysis, writing, or editing that the assignment is intended to assess, it is not.

Violations of this policy will be treated as violations of the course's academic integrity policy.
